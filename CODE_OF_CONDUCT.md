# Code of conduct

These are small projects. This is a short document.

## The rule

Argue about the code, not about the person who wrote it. Everyone here is
spending their own free time, nobody is obliged to answer, and a maintainer
saying "no" to your patch is a decision about scope rather than a verdict on
you.

That applies in every space the projects use: issues, pull requests, review
comments, commit messages and release notes.

## In practice

- Assume the other person read the docs and still got stuck. That is usually
  what happened, and it is often the docs.
- Criticise a change by naming what it breaks. "This is wrong" is not review.
- A question asked twice was not answered clearly the first time.
- Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination and sexual attention are not
  welcome, and neither is the version of them that hides in a joke.
- English or German, both fine. Neither being someone's first language is not
  something to score points off.

## Enforcement

There is one maintainer, so this is not a committee. Comments get edited or
deleted, threads get locked, and accounts get blocked from the repository, in
that order and without much ceremony if the behaviour is deliberate.

To report something privately, use the vulnerability reporting form at
[sapn95/.github](https://github.com/sapn95/.github/security/advisories/new).
It is the only private channel GitHub offers these repositories, and using it
for this is fine — say up front that the report is about conduct rather than a
vulnerability.

## Attribution

The obligations here are the ones from the
[Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org), version 2.1,
written out in fewer words. Where the two disagree, the Covenant is the more
careful text and wins.
